# med spa lead follow up automation: How to Recover After-Hours and Missed-Call Leads Without Adding Front Desk Hours

A Botox inquiry lands at 9:40 on a Tuesday night. Nobody sees it. The front desk calls back Wednesday at 11:20, gets voicemail, and moves on to the patients in the waiting room. By Thursday that lead has booked with the clinic two miles down the road that answered in ninety seconds.

That sequence is the entire reason people search for med spa lead follow up automation. Not because they want "AI" — because they're paying for ads, capturing inquiries, and then losing the handoff somewhere between the form submission and the calendar.

Here's what the automation actually needs to do, what it costs, where it breaks, and how a tool like CloseBot fits into a setup that a med spa can realistically run.

## The five-minute problem

Response speed is the most reliable predictor of whether an aesthetic inquiry becomes a consultation. A 2026 write-up from US Tech Automations on med spa follow-up software cites Lead Management Association benchmark data showing leads contacted within five minutes convert at roughly three times the rate of those contacted after thirty. The same article puts contact rates at 82% for responses under a minute and 29% for responses landing between thirty minutes and four hours.

Whether you accept the exact percentages or not, the direction is hard to argue with. A self-pay aesthetic decision is usually made in parallel — the prospect has three tabs open, and the first clinic to reply has the advantage.

The structural problem isn't laziness at the front desk. It's that treatment rooms eat 30 to 60 minutes at a stretch. Your staff physically cannot watch a form inbox at 9:40pm, and hiring someone to do it is a $40,000+ decision that doesn't scale with ad spend.

So the job description for follow-up automation is narrow and specific:

- Reply to a new inquiry in under a minute, at any hour
- Ask enough qualifying questions to know whether it's a consults for injectables, body contouring, or a $6,000 laser package
- Keep going past touch one and touch two, where most manual sequences die
- Book into the calendar the practice already uses
- Leave a record your team can see when they arrive in the morning

Anything that doesn't do those five things is a notification tool, not a follow-up system.

## What a sequence that survives contact with reality looks like

Most med spa sequences stop at one call and one email. The pattern that shows up repeatedly in aesthetics-specific playbooks is a five-touch cadence compressed into the first week:

1. **0–60 seconds:** SMS with a booking link, signed from the practice
2. **~2 hours:** email with content specific to the treatment they asked about — filler inquiry gets filler content, not a generic newsletter
3. **Next business day:** outbound call attempt with a voicemail drop if unanswered
4. **Day 3:** re-engagement text referencing limited consultation availability
5. **Day 7:** closing-the-loop email

Two details matter more than the cadence itself. First, calling from an unrecognized number gets ignored, so outbound attempts need caller ID showing the practice name. Second, service-specific segments outperform one-size-fits-all messaging badly enough that a single generic sequence is often worse than no sequence.

That's the blueprint. The question is what runs it.

## Where an AI setter fits, and where it doesn't

There are three broad ways to buy this:

**A scheduling/EMR platform with messaging built in** (Zenoti, Nextech, Pabau, AestheticsPro). You keep everything in one system, but follow-up logic tends to be shallower and pricing is often per-location or bundled into a bigger contract.

**A communication layer** (Weave, Podium, Emitrr). Strong on missed-call text-back and unified inboxes. Podium runs $249–$449/month for lead follow-up functionality; CallRail's Voice Assist starts around $145/month as an add-on. These are good at the first text and weaker at reasoning through a real conversation.

**A conversational AI agent layered on top of a CRM.** This is the category CloseBot plays in, and it's the one that behaves most like an actual setter rather than an auto-reply.

The distinction matters for med spas specifically. An aesthetic lead usually has questions — "how much is a syringe of filler," "does the consultation cost anything," "can I do this before a wedding in three weeks." A canned text-back that says "thanks, someone will reach out" doesn't answer any of that. An agent that can pull from your FAQ and book a slot does.

## Three compliance details med spas get wrong

**Pre-consultation leads generally aren't PHI yet.** A form fill from someone who hasn't become a patient usually falls outside HIPAA, which is why standard SMS with a clear opt-out is common for first-touch outreach. The moment a patient relationship begins, your messaging posture has to change. State rules vary, and this isn't a place to guess.

**Opt-out has to work unattended.** Any automated messaging needs a functioning STOP that's honored immediately without someone manually processing it. Ask any vendor to show you what happens the second a patient replies STOP.

**Never let staff text from personal phones.** It puts patient information on a device the practice doesn't control and the conversation disappears when that employee leaves. A platform that owns the number keeps everything in one auditable place — and keeps the reply going out when nobody's on shift.

## FAQ

**How fast should a med spa respond to a new lead?**
Under five minutes, and ideally under one. Aesthetics inquiries are self-pay decisions made in the moment, often alongside two or three competing clinics. If you want to measure your own gap, submit a test inquiry through your own ad on a Friday evening and time the reply.

**How many follow-up touches before giving up?**
Five to seven across roughly fourteen days, weighted toward the first 48 hours. Beyond seven unanswered touches, opt-out risk climbs faster than conversion does.

**Is automated SMS to leads legal?**
The mechanics are TCPA compliance — clear opt-out, honoring STOP immediately, and staying within messaging windows — plus whatever your state adds. Once a patient relationship exists, HIPAA-compliant messaging applies instead. Confirm with your compliance counsel rather than a vendor's marketing page.

**Does CloseBot replace my scheduling or EMR software?**
No. It answers and qualifies, then books back into the calendar inside your CRM, or into another calendar through a custom tool with API access. Your treatment records stay where they are.

**Can it handle Instagram and Facebook lead ads?**
Only through your CRM. If your Instagram and Facebook Messenger are connected to your CRM's conversations inbox, the agent can reply there. CloseBot has no standalone Instagram or WhatsApp connection of its own.

**What happens if a lead asks something the agent doesn't know?**
The Smart FAQ feature flags the question and notifies you. Answer it once and CloseBot can follow up with every lead who asked that same question — which, for a med spa fielding the same five questions about pricing and downtime all week, is the feature that quietly saves the most time.
